# Theme 3 — Text-to-Image / Sketch Generation as a Reflective Surface — READ THIS FILE FIRST

This is the load-bearing file for the whole concept. The strongest primary evidence found directly contradicts the marketing framing found everywhere else.

## Primary source #1 — StoryWriter study (fetched and read directly)
**"Exploring the use of AI text-to-image generation to downregulate negative emotions in an expressive writing application"** — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9810434/ [CS: VERIFIED — fetched and read in full]

- Two studies. Study 1: experimental, 388 participants (131 men / 252 women / 4 other, mean age 30.1), between-subjects, negative vs. neutral emotion induction, StoryWriter-with-images vs. control-without-images. Study 2: qualitative remote survey, 54 participants (30 women / 19 men / 5 non-binary, mean age 33.7).
- **Emotion-regulation finding:** modest effect. Under negative-induction, StoryWriter-with-images reduced anger (Cohen's d = **−0.40**) and sadness (d = **−0.63**) vs. control. Anxiety and stress showed **no significant difference**.
- **Mechanism finding:** Study 2 suggests the benefit is attributable to the *writing process itself*, not the generated images.
- **Image accuracy/misrepresentation finding — the critical one for this concept:**
  - **70.37% (38/54)** of Study 2 participants reported the generated images were **irrelevant** to their own narrative.
  - **70.97%** of participants describing the images used negative language ("scary," "unsettling," "creepy").
  - Only **26.63%** of generated images were rated suitable for the writing task.
  - Participants reported actively avoiding looking at images they found "grotesque."

**This is direct, primary, fetched evidence that a machine attempting to visually represent a person's own emotional disclosure frequently gets it wrong in ways users find unsettling, not just inaccurate.** This is the single most important finding for a voice/text-to-doodle concept and should go into the brief's risk section verbatim with its numbers, not softened.

## Primary source #2 — "A Portrait of Emotion: Empowering Self-Expression through AI-Generated Art" (CogSci 2023, arXiv 2304.13324)
[CS: MEDIUM — abstract/summary level only; full PDF text extraction failed, could not verify sample size or full methodology directly]
- Reported finding: participants preferred images generated from descriptions of their **emotions** over images depicting the literal **events** of their narrative — i.e., people want the machine to render the feeling, not the plot.
- Reported limitation: "images that overrepresent specific elements or stereotypes negatively impact AI alignment" — machine defaulting to visual clichés/stereotypes when depicting emotion is named as a specific failure mode.
- Conclusion (author framing, not independently verified): potential for creativity/self-expression applications extending to "mental health education and therapy interventions" — **flag this explicitly: this is the authors reaching toward clinical/therapeutic framing. Do not import that framing into an internal Tier-1 prototype. See File 04 for why.**

## Market/vendor landscape (not evidence, context only — [CS: LOW] across this block, marketing pages)
- **MoodGallery** — converts daily mood check-ins into AI artwork, has social/sharing features (relevant privacy tension: mood-as-art shared socially vs. journal-as-private).
- **Life Note ("Inner Gallery")** — $10.99/month, generates weekly artwork from entries.
- **Journalie** — AI-enhanced journaling, converts entries to "visual stories."
- **StoryWriter** — the same app studied in Primary Source #1 above; note the gap between the academic finding (70% found images irrelevant/unsettling) and how such tools are marketed.
- **MoodMirror (MoodMe)** — framed as "emotional intelligence journal."

**[WOBBLY]** — every vendor page found frames AI-generated mood art as an unambiguous positive feature. The only primary research located that actually tested user reaction to the images themselves (Primary Source #1) found majority-negative reception on relevance and tone. Two data points, in tension, on a topic with almost no independent academic replication. Do not present "people like seeing their mood as art" as settled — it isn't, on the one study that actually measured it.

## What this means for the brief, stated as a gap not a conclusion
No source located tests the specific mechanism this concept proposes — a *doodle* (implied: simple, hand-drawn-feeling, non-photorealistic) generated from a *voice or text journal entry specifically* (not a fictional narrative task, not a mood-slider check-in). The closest analogues (StoryWriter: fiction-writing task; MoodGallery et al.: mood-slider input, not free reflection) are adjacent, not equivalent. **Mark "will a hand-drawn doodle style avoid the 'grotesque/unsettling' reaction found in Primary Source #1" as [?] Unknown** — style (doodle vs. photorealistic vs. generic stock-art) may matter enormously and is untested in what this scout could reach.
