**[WOBBLY]** — The "40+ years, 200+ RCTs, robustly replicated" framing (used heavily in vendor/wellness content, e.g. Mindsera's "200+ Studies Reviewed" page) papers over a real methodological fight: fixed-effects models (Smyth, d=.47) versus the larger, more current random-effects synthesis (Frattaroli, r=.075 — roughly a fifth the size). Frattaroli's own conclusion is that the effect is real but small, and cost-effectiveness is the strongest remaining argument, not magnitude. Treat any single "expressive writing works" citation as a synthesis, not a settled fact, unless it names which meta-analysis and which model. ## Journal/app abandonment — the actual retention data

Primary source, fetched and read directly:

**Scoping review — "When and Why Adults Abandon Lifestyle Behavior and Mental Health Mobile Apps"** — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11694054/ [CS: VERIFIED — fetched directly]
- **18 eligible studies, 525,824 total participants, published 2014–2022**
- Median discontinuation: **70% of users within first 100 days**
- Industry comparator cited within: 66% of health apps, 69% of fitness apps abandoned within 90 days
- App-category abandonment ranges cited within: alcohol apps 95–97%, **mental health apps 89–92%**, physical activity apps 54–75%
- **Six reason categories identified** (22 unique sub-reasons):
  1. Technical/functional issues (device compatibility, data loss, account requirements)
  2. Privacy concerns
  3. Poor user experience (confusing UI, low engagement, perceived ineffectiveness)
  4. Content/features (insufficient personalization, no accountability, low variety)
  5. Time and financial cost (burdensome data entry, unexpected fees)
  6. Evolving user needs/goals (goal achieved, motivation loss, substitution by another app)

This is the strongest single retention-relevant citation in this file. Use category #4 ("blank page," low personalization) and #5 ("burdensome data entry") directly against the doodle-concept's value proposition — lower friction is the theorized lever, but this review doesn't test image-output journaling specifically, so the connection is inferential, not evidenced. [ASSUMPTION — flag to spec]: "lower-friction non-text capture increases retention" is not directly tested anywhere found in this scout pass. It is a plausible extrapolation from category 4/5 above, not a finding.

**Secondary/unverified stat, not relied on:** "87% of journaling app users abandon within 7 days" — sourced only to a Medium personal-essay post (Mario Mosca), not a study. [CS: LOW] — do not carry into brief as a hard number; cite only as "anecdotal industry claim, unverified."
