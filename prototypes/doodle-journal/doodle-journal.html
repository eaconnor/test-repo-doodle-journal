The evidence that argues against this concept

StoryWriter (PMC9810434) is the only primary study located that measured how people react to an AI-generated image of their own emotional disclosure. Qualitative study, n=54:

70.37%(38/54) judged the generated image irrelevant to their own narrative
70.97%described the image in negative language — "scary," "unsettling," "creepy"
26.63%of images rated suitable for the writing task

The study's own mechanism finding: the modest benefit it measured (anger d=−0.40, sadness d=−0.63) is attributable to the writing process, not the images.

TIER 1 · CONCEPT

This prototype tests one half of one named unknown. StoryWriter's finding is two numbers, not one: #13a — 70.37% found the image irrelevant to what they actually said (a semantic-match failure, which a style change does not plausibly fix), and #13b — 70.97% found it unsettling (an affective-register failure, which a hand-drawn style plausibly does address). This prototype speaks only to #13b, the more forgiving half. It does not test — and cannot answer — whether the concept improves retention, reduces friction, or helps anyone with their day. 58.6% of this brief's tagged claims are [A]/[?] (17 of 29, grep-verified; the earlier 57.1%/28 figure was superseded when #13 was split).
